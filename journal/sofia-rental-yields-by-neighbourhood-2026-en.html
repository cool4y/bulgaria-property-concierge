Sofia Rental Yields by Neighbourhood: The Full 2026 Map

The national average hides a gap of more than 2 percentage points between Sofia's strongest and weakest districts. Here's the number that matters - and which neighbourhoods carry the most prestige.

Market 25 September 2026 9 min read
Накратко

Bulgaria's average gross rental yield sits around 4.2% in early 2026 - but behind that figure lies a gap of more than 2 percentage points between Sofia's strongest and weakest neighbourhoods. Location often outweighs every other investment decision you'll make.

If you're considering a Sofia property for rental income or long-term capital preservation, this is the number you actually need to know - not the national average, but the specific figure for the street you're buying on.

What the official data shows

According to Bulgaria's National Statistical Institute (NSI), Sofia housing prices rose 4.9% in Q2 2025 quarter-on-quarter, accelerating to 5.3% in Q3 2025 - an annual pace of roughly 15.4% nationally, among the highest in the EU.

At the same time, Global Property Guide reports a slight decline in the national average gross yield - from 4.34% in Q3 2025 to around 4.19% in early 2026. Fast-rising prices paired with slower-rising rents is exactly why yield compresses nationally even as it stays strong in specific neighbourhoods.

Sofia's most prestigious neighbourhoods

Beyond yield, several Sofia districts carry a status that attracts a different kind of buyer - less a yield-focused investor, more someone buying for status, security and long-term value preservation.

The luxury villa belt: Boyana, Dragalevtsi, Simeonovo

Sitting at the foot of Vitosha mountain, these three districts form the undisputed top of the market. Boyana is home to a state residence and the National Museum of History, and is consistently named Sofia's most elite district - villas and houses here sell in the €500,000 to over €1.5 million range. Dragalevtsi offers a similarly premium but slightly broader price band (€300,000–€600,000 for a typical house, with top-end properties exceeding €1.5M), with a strong emphasis on security and architectural quality. Simeonovo rounds out the belt with a more family-oriented profile - proximity to international schools and the Vitosha cable car makes it a favourite with foreign executives and diplomats.

Central prestige: Lozenets, Oborishte, Ivan Vazov, Doctor's Garden

Lozenets remains Sofia's signature example of neighbourhood upscaling - bordering Borisova Garden and Loven Park, served by the Vitosha metro station, and close to offices, clinics and international schools. Oborishte and the area around Doctor's Garden rank among the most expensive central addresses, favoured by buyers for whom capital preservation matters more than current yield. Ivan Vazov trails closely behind Lozenets in desirability, with prices approaching the central elite tier.

New southern prestige: Krastova Vada and Manastirski Livadi

These two districts represent the newer wave of supply - modern residential complexes priced above €3,000/sqm, aimed at buyers seeking a contemporary urban lifestyle without necessarily the villa profile of Boyana and Dragalevtsi. Neither has metro access yet, which remains a practical consideration.

More affordable neighbourhoods with good transport links

For buyers whose priority is maximum gross yield rather than prestige, Lyulin, Nadezhda and Ovcha Kupel offer some of Sofia's lowest entry prices - with direct metro access that sustains steady tenant demand despite the more affordable price segment. Geo Milev is worth watching - the expected extension of metro line M3 to the district in 2027 has the potential to move prices ahead of completion.

Rental yield for property in Studentski Grad, Sofia

This district shows up consistently across independent analyses with yields around 4.7%–5.1%, driven by steady demand from students and young professionals.

Rental yield for property in Lozenets, Sofia

Lozenets shows the opposite profile - lower current gross yield due to higher entry prices per square metre, but historically faster appreciation and stronger resale liquidity.

Worth noting on the other side of the ledger: typical long-term rental vacancy across Bulgaria's major cities sits around 5%, meaning one lost month of rent per year on a fairly priced unit.

Why "yield" is never a single number

The gross yield figure you see in most reports is only a starting point. Real, net yield depends on three factors that rarely make it into the headline statistics:

Furnishing quality. A well-furnished, move-in-ready unit shortens vacancy periods and supports higher rent - which is exactly why interior design and furnishing are part of the service, not an afterthought.

Management. A rental that isn't actively managed bleeds real yield every month, regardless of what the gross figure says on paper.

Entry price. With Sofia prices rising 5.3% per quarter according to NSI, a six-month delay can cost more than the difference between two neighbourhoods.

How to choose the right district for your goal

If your priority is maximum current yield - Studentski Grad, Lyulin, Druzhba, Nadezhda and Ovcha Kupel offer lower entry prices and steady tenant demand.

If your priority is capital preservation and growth - Lozenets, Iztok, Oborishte and Ivan Vazov have historically recovered faster after market corrections.

If your priority is prestige and lifestyle - Boyana, Dragalevtsi and Simeonovo are unmatched for buyers seeking a villa with a garden, security and mountain air 20–30 minutes from the centre.

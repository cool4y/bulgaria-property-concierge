How a Foreigner Buys Property in Bulgaria: 7 Steps, No Surprises

Your rights depend on your citizenship, and the land under a house is treated differently from the building itself. Here's what that actually means, step by step.

Guides 25 September 2026 7 min read
Накратко

Yes, a foreigner can buy property in Bulgaria - but rights depend on citizenship. EU/EEA citizens buy everything freely, including land. Non-EU citizens buy apartments and buildings freely, but not land in their own name - a Bulgarian company structure is used for a house with its own plot.

Most people who write to us aren't worried about the price. They're worried about what they can't see - a legal status they don't fully understand, a document in a language they don't read, and decisions they have to make from a distance. Fairly so. So this article doesn't promise "easy" - it explains exactly how the system works.

Who can buy what: EU vs non-EU

Bulgaria's Constitution (Article 22) sets the baseline rule: foreigners and foreign legal entities generally cannot acquire ownership of land, except under an international treaty, by inheritance by operation of law, or - for EU and EEA citizens - on equal terms with Bulgarian nationals.

In practice, this means:

EU and EEA citizens can freely buy apartments, buildings and land - including regulated plots - directly in their own name, without restriction.

Non-EU citizens can freely buy apartments and buildings, but cannot own land personally. Where a property includes its own plot - a house with a garden, a villa, a building plot - the standard solution is a Bulgarian company (most often an EOOD) that holds the property.

Apartments in buildings under shared ownership fall outside this restriction, because the land beneath them is a common part of the building - which is exactly why apartments remain the simplest entry point for non-EU investors.

What buying property does NOT give you

An important point rarely mentioned: buying property does not automatically grant a right of residence or a visa. Ownership can support a long-stay (Type D) visa application, but that requires a separate, standalone procedure with its own financial and documentary conditions.

The seven steps of the purchase

1. Define your budget and goal

Yield, capital growth, a personal home, or a mix of the three - your choice of district and property type follows directly from this. See our Sofia rental yield analysis by neighbourhood as a starting point.

2. Search and viewing

For buyers abroad, this often happens through an organised VIP tour - a compressed, efficient viewing of pre-shortlisted properties instead of weeks of independent searching.

3. Reservation and preliminary contract

Once a property is chosen, a preliminary contract is signed with a deposit, setting out price, timelines, and exactly what's included in the deal.

4. Legal due diligence

This is the step that saves or sinks a deal. We cover it in detail in the next guide in this series.

5. Setting up a company (only if needed)

If the buyer is a non-EU citizen and the property includes land, the company is registered at this stage - before the notarial deed, not after.

6. The notarial deed

The final transfer takes place before a notary in the district where the property is located. Since Bulgaria's 2026 euro adoption, payment can be made directly in euro.

7. Registration and post-purchase steps

The deed is registered with the Registry Agency, followed by a tax declaration with the municipality, and - for investment purposes - setting up rental management.

What the deal actually costs

Total transaction costs typically fall in the 3–6% range of the property value. Based on our own experience with clients, the typical total sits closer to the lower end of that range - around 4–5% - on a well-structured, uncomplicated deal. See our FAQ answer for the exact breakdown.

Getting a mortgage as a foreigner

Bulgaria's major banks lend to foreigners, though terms differ from those for resident borrowers. Since Bulgaria's 2026 euro adoption, currency risk on loan repayments is eliminated for borrowers earning in euro.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreigner buy an apartment in Bulgaria?+
Yes, without restriction - both EU and non-EU citizens can freely buy apartments and buildings. The restriction applies only to direct land ownership by non-EU citizens.
Do I need to register a company to buy property in Bulgaria?+
Only if you are a non-EU citizen buying a property that includes its own land. No company is needed for an apartment in a shared-ownership building.
Does buying property grant residency in Bulgaria?+
Not automatically. Ownership can support a long-stay residence application, but it requires a separate procedure.
How much do total transaction costs come to?+
Typically between 3% and 6% of the property value. On a well-structured deal, the real cost is often closer to 4–5%.
What documents does a foreigner need for the notarial deed?+
A valid passport and a Bulgarian personal identification number for foreigners, issued by the National Revenue Agency for tax and property purposes. If buying through a company, the EOOD registration documents are added. Banks also require proof of the source of funds.
